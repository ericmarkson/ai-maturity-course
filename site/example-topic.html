The Confidence Dial: How a Decision Threshold Changes a Classifier's Answer

Learning objective: Learner can explain why moving a decision threshold changes which side of a boundary a fixed confidence score falls on, without the underlying score itself changing.

Definition

Many classifiers don't just output "yes" or "no" — they output a confidence score (e.g. "72% likely spam"), and a separate decision threshold converts that score into a final label. Raise the threshold and fewer items get labeled positive; lower it and more do — even though no score changed.

Subtopics

  • Confidence score vs. final label — two different things
  • Why raising the threshold trades fewer false positives for more false negatives
  • Why the "right" threshold depends on which mistake costs more in context
