Advising Policy and Governance on Frontier AI

Learning objective: Given a specific frontier-AI risk scenario, draft a one-page policy recommendation that names the governance lever (disclosure requirement, compute threshold, audit mandate, etc.) best suited to it and explain why a different lever would be less effective.

Definition

Advising policy and governance on frontier AI means translating technical realities about frontier models into guidance usable by regulators, legislators, and international bodies — people who must make binding decisions without necessarily having deep technical background, under real uncertainty about what a given model can actually do.

Subtopics

  • The current governance landscape — the EU's risk-tiered prescriptive model, the US's voluntary-standards-plus-state-patchwork model (NIST AI RMF, state laws), and China's centrally administered filing/review model.
  • Frontier-specific governance tools — capability/compute thresholds for triggering oversight, frontier safety frameworks, and internal audit functions at AI developers.
  • The policymaker's information problem — frontier AI is built by a small number of firms, but capabilities and risks are hard for outsiders to verify independently.
  • International coordination efforts and their limits given competitive dynamics between states and labs.
