Frontier Safety Cases, Threat Modeling & Alignment Evaluation

Learning objective: Construct a structured frontier safety case (Claim → Argument → Evidence) for a dangerous capability threshold, audit it for single points of failure, and design elicitation tests that mitigate sandbagging and jailbreak recovery.

Definition

A frontier safety case is a structured, auditable, and defeasible technical argument demonstrating why deploying or releasing a frontier AI model remains acceptably safe against severe threat vectors (such as autonomous cyber exploitation, CBRN uplift, or autonomous self-exfiltration) given specific capability evaluations and layered mitigations.

How It Works

Borrowed from aviation and nuclear assurance, modern Responsible Scaling Policies (RSPs) and Preparedness Frameworks replace ad-hoc "red-team vibes" with formal risk gates:

  • Claims → Arguments → Evidence: A top-level claim (e.g., "Model X cannot automate end-to-end zero-day cyberattacks") breaks into sub-claims supported by concrete arguments (either "the model lacks the latent capability even when elicited" or "deployment mitigations prevent exploitation") backed by reproducible evidence (eval logs, weight security audits, classifier ROC curves).
  • Capability thresholds: Before scaling training compute or deploying externally, labs run dangerous-capability evals against predefined red lines (ASL-3/High thresholds). Crossing a threshold freezes release until stronger mitigations are verified.
  • Defense-in-depth layers: Safe deployment never relies on a single gate. Four orthogonal layers reinforce one another: (1) pretraining filtering / unlearning to reduce hazardous knowledge density, (2) refusal & circuit-breaker post-training to steer policy behavior, (3) runtime I/O monitors & anomaly classifiers to catch multi-turn jailbreaks, and (4) access controls & weight security (KYC, rate limits, airgapped weights, egress sandboxing).
  • Elicitation & sandbagging defense: Zero-shot prompting routinely underestimates dangerous capabilities. Proper capability evaluations use agentic scaffolding, tool access, and helpful-only fine-tuning (adversarial elicitation) to strip away refusal conditioning, alongside noise-injection or linear probes to detect intentional evaluation sandbagging.

Common Pitfall

Single-point-of-failure safety cases: Treating refusal training (RLHF/DPO) as sufficient protection when a model crosses a dangerous capability threshold. In practice, a few dozen fine-tuning steps on open weights—or universal multi-turn jailbreaks on an unmonitored API—can strip refusal alignment while leaving underlying dangerous knowledge intact.
