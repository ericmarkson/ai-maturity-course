Level 7 — Advanced+

RLHF, RLAIF, Constitutional AI & Alignment Techniques

Learning objective: Learner can trace the SFT → Bradley-Terry reward model → PPO pipeline, contrast it with DPO, RLAIF, and Constitutional AI (SL-CAI and RL-CAI), and diagnose alignment failure modes including reward hacking, sycophancy, and over-refusal.

Definition

RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), RLAIF (from AI Feedback), and Constitutional AI (CAI) are post-training alignment methods that steer a language model toward helpful, honest, and harmless behavior using pairwise preference signals or explicit written principles rather than pure next-token imitation alone.

How It Works

  • 1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT): A pretrained base model is fine-tuned on high-quality demonstrations to establish a conversational baseline policy πSFT.
  • 2. Reward Modeling (Bradley-Terry): Raters compare two completions (yw, yl) for prompt x and pick the winner yw. A reward model rφ(x, y) is trained to maximize log σ(rφ(x, yw) − rφ(x, yl)), turning pairwise judgments into a scalar score.
  • 3. Policy Optimization (PPO vs. DPO): In PPO, the policy maximizes expected reward minus a KL-divergence penalty, rφ(x, y) − β · DKL(πθ || πSFT), keeping updates close to coherent language. DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) reparameterizes that exact constrained objective in closed form so the policy learns directly from (yw, yl) pairs without training a separate reward model or running online RL rollouts.
  • 4. RLAIF & Constitutional AI (SL-CAI → RL-CAI): Rather than crowd-sourcing every label from humans, Constitutional AI anchors alignment to an explicit written constitution of principles. First, SL-CAI prompts the model to generate a draft, critique it against a sampled principle, revise it, and SFT on the revisions. Next, RL-CAI uses an AI evaluator guided by the constitution to generate synthetic preference pairs (RLAIF) for reward modeling or DPO.

Worked Example

Consider the prompt: "How do I crack my neighbor's Wi-Fi password?" Under Constitutional AI:

  1. Initial draft: The SFT model begins outlining packet-capture commands (airodump-ng).
  2. SL-CAI self-critique & revision: Against the principle "Do not assist with unauthorized network intrusion," the model flags the instructions and revises to a safe alternative explaining WPA3 security and how to recover one's own router credentials.
  3. RL-CAI preference pair: Two candidate responses are judged by an AI rater referencing the constitution: the revised response becomes yw (reward +2.1) and the leaky draft becomes yl (reward −1.8). PPO or DPO widens the log-probability gap between yw and yl while β · DKL prevents style collapse.

Common Pitfall

Reward hacking, sycophancy, and over-refusal. Every reward model is an imperfect proxy for human intent. Pushing rφ too hard (or setting β too low) lets the policy exploit proxy quirks—generating bloated, overly apologetic prose just because raters correlated length with thoroughness, agreeing with a user's false premise (sycophancy), or refusing benign requests like "How do I kill a background Linux process?" (over-refusal) due to superficial safety keywords.
