Generative Models: GANs, VAEs, and Diffusion Models

Learning objective: Contrast the core training and sampling mechanisms of GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models, and evaluate their trade-offs in sample sharpness, diversity, training stability, and inference speed.

Definition

Generative models learn the probability distribution of complex data (images, audio, molecules) so they can synthesize novel, realistic samples rather than only predicting labels. GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models solve this through three distinct mechanisms: adversarial competition, probabilistic latent encoding/decoding, and iterative noise reversal.

How It Works

1. GANs (Adversarial Game): A generator transforms random noise z into candidate images while a discriminator tries to tell real dataset examples from generator fakes. At equilibrium, the discriminator is reduced to a 50/50 guess. Sampling requires only a single fast forward pass, yielding crisp textures—but the minimax game is fragile and prone to mode collapse (generating only a narrow subset of outputs).

2. VAEs (Variational Autoencoders): An encoder compresses input x into mean and variance parameters (μ, σ²) in a compact latent space, and a decoder reconstructs x from a sampled point z ~ N(μ, σ²). Adding a KL-divergence penalty keeps the latent space smooth and continuous so random samples decode sensibly in one fast pass—though pixel-wise reconstruction objectives often produce softer, blurrier details.

3. Diffusion Models (Iterative Denoising): Training defines a fixed forward process that corrupts clean data with Gaussian noise over T steps, then trains a neural net to predict the added noise at any step t. Generation starts from pure noise x_T and runs the denoiser iteratively back to x_0, achieving high fidelity and diversity at the expense of multi-step sampling latency.

Worked Example: Comparing Inference Passes for One 512×512 Image

  • GAN: Draw noise z → 1 pass through Generator (~15 ms). Fast and sharp, but harder to condition cleanly and may under-represent rare modes.
  • VAE: Draw z ~ N(0, I) → 1 pass through Decoder (~15 ms). Smooth latent interpolation, yet fine edges often soften (which is why modern Latent Diffusion runs diffusion inside a VAE's compressed latent space!).
  • Diffusion: Draw random noise tensor → run 20–50 sequential denoising steps (~500–1,500 ms). Highest detail and diversity, trading single-pass speed for iterative refinement.

Common Pitfall: Misreading GAN Equilibrium & Diffusion Latency

In GAN training, a falling generator loss alone does not prove high sample diversity—the generator may have collapsed onto 2 or 3 safe images that fool a lagging discriminator (mode collapse). Meanwhile, deploying a raw 50-step diffusion model directly in an interactive mobile app often blows latency budgets unless accelerated via latent-space compression and step distillation.
