Level 5 — Intermediate+

Generative Models: GANs, VAEs, and Diffusion Models

Learning objective: Learner can distinguish the core generation mechanism of a GAN (adversarial generator vs. discriminator), a VAE (encode to a latent distribution, then decode/sample), and a diffusion model (iterative denoising), and can describe at a high level what "generating a sample" looks like for each.

Definition

A generative model learns the underlying patterns in a set of training data (images, text, audio, etc.) well enough to produce brand-new, realistic-looking samples — rather than just labeling or predicting something about existing data. GANs, VAEs, and diffusion models are three different mechanisms for doing this, each with its own training dynamics and trade-offs between sample quality, diversity, and training stability.

Subtopics

  • GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks) — a generator network tries to produce fake samples realistic enough to fool a discriminator network, while the discriminator tries to tell real from fake; the two improve together in an adversarial game.
  • VAEs (Variational Autoencoders) — an encoder compresses input data into a compact probability distribution (the latent space), and a decoder samples from that distribution to reconstruct or generate new data.
  • Diffusion models — trained to gradually add noise to data step by step, then learn to reverse that process, so generation starts from pure random noise and "denoises" it over many steps into a realistic sample.
  • Trade-offs — GANs can produce sharp samples but are notoriously unstable to train; VAEs train more stably but samples can be blurrier; diffusion models tend to produce high-quality, diverse samples but generation requires many sequential steps.
