Transformer Architecture and Attention

Learning objective: Explain, in plain language and with a diagram, how a transformer decides which earlier words matter most when producing the next word, and why this replaced RNNs for most large-scale NLP.

Definition

A transformer is a neural network design that processes an entire sequence at once using attention to weigh how much every other token should influence each token, instead of reading one word at a time like older recurrent models (RNNs). Attention lets the model directly connect "it" in a sentence back to the noun it refers to, even many words earlier, without passing information step by step through every word in between.

Subtopics

  • Self-attention and the query/key/value mechanism
  • Multi-head attention (why multiple attention "views" help)
  • Encoder vs. decoder blocks and encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only designs
  • Why transformers parallelize better than RNNs (no sequential bottleneck)
