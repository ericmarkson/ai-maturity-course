Level 5 — Intermediate+

Pretrained Language Models (BERT/GPT-style)

Learning objective: Match a language model task to a BERT-style (masked bidirectional encoder) or GPT-style (autoregressive causal decoder) pretraining objective, and explain why pretrain-then-adapt outperforms training from scratch.

Definition

Pretrained language models learn broad syntax, semantics, and world knowledge once via self-supervised training on massive unlabeled corpora, then adapt cheaply to downstream tasks. BERT-style encoders train with masked language modeling (MLM)—predicting masked tokens using bidirectional context from both sides—excelling at understanding, classification, and retrieval. GPT-style decoders train with causal next-token prediction—predicting the next token from left context only—excelling at open-ended generation and instruction following.

How It Works

1. Bidirectional MLM (BERT-style): During pretraining, roughly 15% of input tokens are masked or corrupted (e.g., "The [MASK] barked loudly"). Because encoder self-attention has no causal mask, every token reads both preceding and following words to reconstruct the missing token. This makes encoders ideal for sentiment classification, named-entity extraction, and dense sentence embeddings.

2. Autoregressive Next-Token Prediction (GPT-style): Every token predicts the immediately following token using a causal mask so future tokens remain hidden. At inference time, the model generates one token, appends it to the prompt, and repeats—turning a single pretraining objective into a general-purpose text generator and zero/few-shot reasoner.

3. Pretraining, Scale & Downstream Adaptation: Instead of learning grammar and facts from a small labeled dataset (which overfits quickly), practitioners start from small/base/large pretrained checkpoints. Larger parameter counts retain richer long-tail facts and compositional patterns, though smaller distilled encoders still dominate low-latency classification pipelines.

Worked Example: Why Right Context Resolves Ambiguity

Consider predicting the blank in "I picked up the hot ____ before my meeting." At the blank position, a GPT-style decoder only sees "I picked up the hot", so probability spreads across "coffee", "pizza", "pan", or "laptop". Now change the sentence ending to "I picked up the hot ____ and dialed the client." A BERT-style encoder sees "dialed the client" to the right of the blank and shifts probability sharply toward "phone"—whereas a causal model couldn't read those future tokens at that step.

Common Pitfall: Mismatching Architecture to Task

Using an out-of-the-box GPT-style decoder for fast document classification or sentence embeddings often adds unnecessary latency and weaker early-token representations (since early tokens cannot attend to the end of the sentence). Conversely, trying to force a bidirectional BERT encoder to generate long, coherent multi-paragraph prose breaks down because it was never trained to sample sequences step-by-step left-to-right.
