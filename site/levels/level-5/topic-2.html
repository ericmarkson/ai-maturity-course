Pretrained Language Models (BERT/GPT-style)

Learning objective: Correctly classify a given model or task as better suited to a BERT-style vs. GPT-style pretraining objective, and explain why.

Definition

Pretrained language models are transformers trained once on huge amounts of general text so they already "know" language patterns, then reused for many different tasks instead of training from scratch each time. BERT-style models are trained with masked language modeling — predict a hidden word using context from both sides — which makes them strong at understanding tasks. GPT-style models are trained with next-token prediction — predict the next word using only the words before it — which makes them naturally suited to open-ended text generation.

Subtopics

  • Masked language modeling (BERT-style, bidirectional understanding) vs. next-token prediction (GPT-style, autoregressive generation)
  • Pretraining vs. downstream task adaptation
  • Model families and scale (small/base/large variants, why parameter count matters)
  • Why "pretrained + adapt" beat training every model from scratch
