Level 3 — Prompt Engineering

Breaking Big Tasks Down: Step-by-Step & Chained Prompting

Learning objective: Recognize when one prompt is trying to juggle too many jobs at once, split it into a clean 2–3 step prompt chain, and pass verified intermediate artifacts cleanly between steps.

Definition

Prompt chaining (task decomposition) splits one overloaded multi-part request into a sequence of smaller, single-purpose prompts—where each step emits a clean intermediate artifact (like verified JSON facts, a risk list, or a draft) that becomes the focused input to the next step.

How It Works (Why Mega-Prompts Drop the Ball)

Asking one mega-prompt to simultaneously read messy notes, verify dates, weigh tradeoffs, write polished customer prose, critique itself, and return strict JSON spreads the model's attention across five competing jobs. Middle rules silently drop, internal scratch notes leak into customer prose, and when an error slips into the output, you cannot isolate which sub-skill failed.

Splitting the work into a 3-step chain gives each model call one clear job:

Chain Stage Single Focus Clean Output Artifact Why Isolation Helps
Step 1: Extract & Verify Pull facts, dates, metrics, or clauses from raw noise Structured JSON or bullet fact sheet Zero storytelling—easy to inspect against source text
Step 2: Analyze / Draft Turn verified facts into audience-ready draft or risk rating Focused prose draft or scored table Raw transcript noise never distracts Step 2 wording
Step 3: Audit & Format Verify constraints (length, banned words, schema) & polish Final validated deliverable Separates creation from strict quality checking
  • Pass clean artifacts, not full chat history: Start Step 2 with only the verified Step 1 JSON rather than re-pasting the messy 4-page transcript and prior chatter.
  • Add a Review Gate when stakes are high: Checking Step 1 facts or Step 2 drafts (via human scan or code rule) stops small misreads from polishing into confident mistakes at Step 3.

Worked Example: Feature Spec → Launch Email

  • Overloaded Mega-Prompt (1 call): "Read this 4-page RFC, extract public features, ignore internal codenames, draft a 110-word customer launch email, and check no unconfirmed dates appear." → Frequently leaks internal codename Project Kestrel, invents a May launch date from a draft comment, and runs 195 words.
  • 3-Step Chain: Step 1 extracts {"public_features": ["CSV Bulk Export"], "confirmed_date": null, "caveats": ["Beta for Pro plans"]}. Step 2 drafts the email from that clean JSON alone (so Project Kestrel doesn't even exist in Step 2's prompt!). Step 3 checks word count (≤110 words) and replaces any date guess with "rolling out this month".

Common Pitfall: Over-Chaining Simple Tasks & Forwarding Dirty History

Do not split a simple 1-step rewrite (like polishing a two-sentence Slack message) into four chained calls—each hop adds latency and cost. And when you do chain, avoid dumping the entire noisy raw source + chat history into Steps 2 and 3; dragging dirty context forward re-introduces the exact distraction chaining was built to eliminate.
