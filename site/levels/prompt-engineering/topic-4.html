Level 3 — Prompt Engineering

Controlling What It Outputs: Formats, Templates, Tone & Length

Learning objective: Specify exact output contracts—using Markdown templates, tables, strict JSON keys, numeric length caps, tone anchors, and no-preamble rules—so AI replies arrive copy-paste-ready or safely parseable on every run.

Definition

An output contract defines the exact shape, voice, and length budget of an AI reply before the model starts generating tokens—turning unpredictable prose into consistent, copy-ready Markdown or machine-parseable JSON.

How It Works (Five Controls Over Output Shape)

  • Exact Markdown templates for human workflows: When you paste notes into Slack, Notion, or email, supply an explicit skeleton with headings and slot labels (e.g., ## Decisions, - [Owner] — [Task] (Due: [Date])). The model fills your slots rather than inventing its own layout.
  • Markdown tables vs. Strict JSON keys: Use a Markdown table with named columns when humans scan comparisons side by side. Use Strict JSON with exact key names, types, and allowed enum values ("priority": "low" | "medium" | "high") when code, webhooks, or spreadsheets parse the output.
  • Numeric length caps instead of adjectives: Replace fuzzy words like "brief" or "concise" with hard counts: "Max 3 bullets, ≤12 words per bullet" or "1 summary sentence (≤25 words)."
  • Concrete audience & tone anchors: Anchor register to a recognizable role and style ("Blunt executive brief," "Calm support lead," "Neutral machine-readable") rather than generic requests like "sound professional."
  • No conversational preamble or outro: Explicitly add "Output ONLY the template/JSON—no greeting, commentary, or closing remarks." This strips "Sure! Here is your summary:" and makes replies immediately pasteable or valid for JSON.parse().

Worked Example: Reusable Output Contract Skeleton

Audience & Tone: VP of Engineering — direct, factual, zero fluff.
Length Cap: Exactly 3 bullets under Action Items, ≤14 words per bullet.
Rule: Output ONLY the template below. No intro sentence, no sign-off.

### Weekly Release Summary
- Status: [On Track | At Risk | Blocked]
- Top Blocker: [1 sentence, ≤18 words, or "None"]

### Action Items
- [Owner] | [Action] | [Due Date]

Common Pitfall: Trusting Subjective Words Like "Brief" or "Nice Format"

To one training document, "brief" means 2 sentences; to another, it means a 4-paragraph executive memo. Asking for a "nice, brief summary" guarantees inconsistent formatting across runs—and omitting a no-preamble rule lets chat wrappers ("Certainly! Here's what I found:") break automated JSON parsers.
