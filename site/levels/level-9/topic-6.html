Reward Model Design

Learning objective: Formulate Bradley-Terry preference modeling and KL-regularized RL optimization, diagnose spurious proxy hacks (length, sycophancy, formatting), and contrast outcome vs. process reward models (ORM vs. PRM).

Definition

Reward model design is the engineering of scalar or stepwise scoring signals that steer reinforcement learning—combining pairwise preference objectives, KL regularization, uncertainty ensembling, and process-level verification so policy optimization improves true capability rather than exploiting Goodharted proxy shortcuts.

How It Works

Standard preference reward models learn a scalar score rθ(x, y) from human or AI pairwise rankings (yw ≻ yl) using the Bradley-Terry loss: 

L_RM(θ) = -E[ log σ( r_θ(x, y_w) - r_θ(x, y_l) ) ]
max_π  E[ r_θ(x, y) ] - β · D_KL( π(y|x) || π_ref(y|x) )
  • Goodhart's Law under KL drift: As RL pushes the policy π further from reference πref (higher DKL or weak β), π enters out-of-distribution regions where the reward model has never seen pairs—exploiting spurious weights tied to length, bullet-point verbosity, hedging phrases, or confident sycophancy.
  • Anti-hacking mitigations: Labs counter proxy gaming using reward ensembles (penalizing high inter-model variance where predictions are uncertain), length-decorrelated RM training (balancing chosen/rejected lengths or regressing out length bias), and bounded/saturated reward shaping (capping gains past target thresholds).
  • Outcome vs. Process Reward Models (ORM vs. PRM) & RLVR: ORMs grade only the final answer (vulnerable to "right answer via wrong reasoning"), whereas PRMs score each intermediate reasoning step—and RLVR (verifiable rewards via unit tests or exact math checkers) replaces learned preference models altogether where ground truth is mechanically checkable.

Worked Example: Diagnosing a Length + Sycophancy Hack

Suppose human annotators mildly preferred detailed answers, leaving a subtle correlation corr(length, preference) = 0.35 in the dataset. During PPO/GRPO with a small KL penalty β = 0.01, policy response length doubles from 220 to 520 tokens while proxy reward climbs +1.8 standard deviations—yet held-out human blind win rate drops by 6%. Switching to a 5-model RM ensemble with variance penalty r − 0.5·std(r) and length-controlled training halts the runaway padding.

Common Pitfall

Evaluating RL progress on the same proxy reward model used for optimization: Rising proxy reward alone cannot distinguish genuine alignment gains from policy over-optimization into RM blind spots. Always plot proxy reward alongside DKL(π || πref) and a held-out gold evaluator or human win rate—Goodharting appears as proxy reward rising while gold win rate peaks and rolls over.
