Synthetic Data Generation and Curation

Learning objective: Architect synthetic-data pipelines using rejection sampling, Magpie/Evol-Instruct, and deduplication while preventing model collapse (tail truncation and distributional narrowing) through grounded real-data anchoring.

Definition

Synthetic data generation and curation is the automated synthesis, verification, and diversity filtering of model-produced training examples—scaling high-signal instruction, reasoning, and tool trajectories beyond scarce human annotations without triggering recursive distributional collapse.

How It Works

High-yield synthetic pipelines treat generation and filtering as a coupled system:

  • Verification-gated rejection sampling: Rather than distilling raw completions, frontier pipelines sample k rollouts per prompt and keep only trajectories verified by deterministic unit tests, math CAS solvers, or reward-model ensembles—converting inference compute into clean post-training data.
  • Magpie & Evol-Instruct diversity synthesis: Evol-Instruct iteratively mutates seed prompts along depth (constraints, multi-hop reasoning) and breadth dimensions. Magpie feeds an aligned chat template prefix (e.g., <|user|>) directly to an autoregressive model to sample fresh, unseeded user queries across long-tail topics.
  • Model collapse dynamics: Shumailov et al. showed that repeatedly training generation t+1 purely on generation t's uncurated samples causes early model collapse (low-probability tail concepts vanish due to finite sampling variance) followed by late collapse (distribution variance σt2 → 0 onto a few repetitive modes).
  • Dedup & real-data anchoring: MinHash LSH (lexical) plus embedding cosine deduplication strips near-duplicate synthetic boilerplate, while retaining a non-zero fraction of fresh human/real data each round anchors variance and tail coverage.

Worked Example: Anchoring Recursive Self-Distillation

Suppose a base distribution has spread σ0 = 30 and each unanchored generation contracts spread by 0.8×. After 5 purely synthetic generations (0% real re-injection), σ5 = 30 × 0.85 = 9.8 (33% of original diversity—tails wiped out). Re-injecting just 25% real curated samples each round converges to a steady-state spread of ~18.8 (63% retained), keeping rare edge cases alive while still gaining 4× synthetic scale.

Common Pitfall

Over-pruning with narrow quality classifiers: Filtering out the lowest-scoring 80% of synthetic outputs with a single perplexity or RM threshold raises per-sample cleanliness while inadvertently discarding legitimate stylistic, domain, and difficulty diversity—leaving downstream models brittle outside narrow boilerplate templates.
