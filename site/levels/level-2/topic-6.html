Overfitting vs. Underfitting

Learning objective: Diagnose whether a model is underfit, well-fit, or overfit from both curve visuals and train/test accuracy scores, and pick the right corrective step for each failure mode.

Definition

Underfitting happens when a model is too simple to capture the real pattern, scoring poorly even on training data. Overfitting happens when an overly flexible model memorizes random noise in the training set—scoring great in training while failing on unseen test data. A well-fit model matches the true trend cleanly across both.

How It Works (The Study Habit Analogy)

  • Underfitting = Barely skimming chapter titles: You spend 5 minutes glancing at bold headings and fail both the practice quiz and the real test. Fix: add model flexibility/features or train longer.
  • Overfitting = Memorizing exact word-for-word phrasing: You memorize "Question 4's answer is C" without understanding why, acing the practice sheet (99%) while bombing a reworded final exam (58%). Fix: gather more varied data, simplify the model, or add regularization.
  • Just right = Learning the underlying rule: You do well on practice questions (89%) and almost just as well on fresh test questions (87%).

Worked Example: Reading the Numbers

  • Train error = 42, Test error = 45 → Both high → Underfitting (curve too rigid/flat).
  • Train error = 1, Test error = 65 → Huge gap → Overfitting (curve wiggles wildly through noise).
  • Train error = 8, Test error = 10 → Both low & close → Well-fit.

Common Pitfall: Chasing 100% Training Score

Real-world data always contains natural randomness (two identical houses rarely sell for the exact same dollar). Pushing complexity until training error hits zero almost guarantees your model started memorizing noise rather than signal—always watch the gap between training and validation/test error!
