Level 2 — Novice

Basic Prompt Engineering

Learning objective: Turn a vague, open-ended prompt into a crisp structured prompt using role, context, task, format, and few-shot examples—and iterate effectively on the first draft.

Definition

Prompt engineering is writing clear, unambiguous instructions—combining role, context, task, and output format (plus optional examples)—so a language model narrows straight in on what you need instead of guessing from millions of generic possibilities.

How It Works (Briefing a Smart New Teammate)

Imagine handing a skilled new intern a sticky note reading "Write about dogs." Should they write a veterinary textbook, a bedtime story for kids, or a social post? An AI faces the exact same ambiguity when you underspecify. Specifying four anchors removes the guesswork:

  • Role: Who the model should act like ("You are a pediatric veterinarian").
  • Context: Audience and constraints ("Writing a handout for 8-year-old pet owners").
  • Task: Exact job to perform ("Explain 5 essential puppy care tips").
  • Format (+ Few-Shot Examples): Exact output shape ("5 numbered bullets, under 15 words each") plus 1–2 examples showing your exact tone or schema.

Worked Example: Vague vs. Structured Rewrite

  • Vague prompt: "Summarize this customer call." → Often returns a rambling 3-paragraph essay missing action items.
  • Structured prompt: "Act as a QA team lead (Role) preparing a daily CRM note (Context). Summarize the transcript below (Task) into a 3-row Markdown table with columns Issue, Sentiment, and Next Action (Format), keeping each cell under 12 words (Constraint)." → Instantly usable output!

Common Pitfall: Expecting One-Shot Perfection & Relying on Vague Adjectives

Asking an AI to "make it better and punchier" leaves room for wildly different interpretations. Show one or two short few-shot examples of the exact style you want, treat the first output as a rough draft, and refine whichever constraint missed the mark.
