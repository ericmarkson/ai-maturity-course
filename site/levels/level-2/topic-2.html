Level 2 — Novice

Basic Probability and Statistics for ML

Learning objective: Interpret model confidence percentages as probabilities rather than guarantees, distinguish center (mean/median) from spread (variance), and spot correlation without mistaking it for causation.

Definition

Machine learning runs on uncertainty rather than absolute rules. Probability expresses how likely an outcome is on a 0 to 1 (or 0%–100%) scale; mean, median, and variance summarize where data centers and how widely it spreads; and correlation measures whether two variables tend to rise or fall together—which never by itself proves one causes the other.

How It Works (Everyday Intuition)

  • Model outputs are weather forecasts, not promises: Saying "80% chance of rain" means that across days with similar radar patterns, it rains about 8 out of 10 times—not that it definitely will rain today.
  • Center vs. Spread: Two classrooms can both have an average (mean) test score of 75, yet one has almost everyone scoring 72–78 (low variance) while the other mixes 50s and 100s (high variance). Knowing only the average hides how predictable individual points are.
  • Correlation vs. Causation: Variables often move together because of a hidden shared driver (confounder)—like summer heat driving both ice cream sales and swimming pool attendance.

Worked Example: Reading "87% Confidence This Is Spam"

Suppose your inbox receives 100 messages that your spam filter scores around 87%. If the model is well-calibrated, roughly 87 of those emails truly are spam, and about 13 are legitimate messages that happened to contain spam-like words (like "urgent invoice" or "free upgrade"). That's why email apps route borderline flags to a Spam folder instead of permanently deleting them!

Common Pitfall: Assuming Mean Tells the Whole Story (or Acting on Spurious Correlations)

A single billionaire walking into a coffee shop skyrockets the mean income inside the room even though everyone else's pay stayed identical—which is why median (the middle value) is often safer when outliers exist. Similarly, building a product feature around a spurious correlation (e.g., sending free umbrellas thinking it stops rain) fails whenever the hidden real cause stays untouched.
