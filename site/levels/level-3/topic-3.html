Level 3 — Advanced Beginner

k-NN and k-Means Clustering

Learning objective: Given a 2D scatterplot and a new point, you should be able to manually identify its nearest neighbors and predict a k-NN classification, and separately describe what one "round" of k-means does to a set of cluster centers.

Definition

k-NN (k-nearest neighbors) classifies a new point by looking at its k closest labeled neighbors and taking a majority vote among their classes. k-means groups unlabeled points into k clusters by repeatedly assigning each point to its nearest cluster center, then recomputing each center as the average of the points assigned to it, until the centers stop moving.

Subtopics

  • Distance metrics (Euclidean distance, intuitively) — "closeness" between two points as straight-line distance.
  • Choosing k and the bias/variance tradeoff it creates — small k is jumpy and sensitive to noise; large k smooths things out but can blur real boundaries.
  • k-means initialization, assignment, and update loop — the repeating cycle of "assign to nearest center, then move the center."
  • Supervised (k-NN) vs. unsupervised (k-means) — k-NN needs labeled classes to vote with; k-means discovers structure with no labels at all.
