Level 3 — Advanced Beginner

Gradient Descent

Learning objective: You should be able to describe, in your own words, what happens at each step of gradient descent, and predict qualitatively what goes wrong if the learning rate is set too high or too low.

Definition

Gradient descent is the algorithm that trains most ML models by repeatedly nudging the model's parameters in the direction that most reduces the model's error. At every step it looks at the slope (the "gradient") of the loss at the current position and takes a small step in the downhill direction — over many steps, this walks the parameters toward a point where the loss is low.

Subtopics

  • The loss landscape as a hilly surface — you can picture every possible setting of a model's parameters as a point on a landscape, where the height at that point is how wrong the model is. Gradient descent is just walking downhill on that landscape.
  • Learning rate: too small vs. too large — the learning rate controls how big each downhill step is. Too small and training crawls along, wasting time; too large and each step can fly past the valley floor and land higher up the other side (overshoot), or even bounce further and further away (diverge).
  • Local minima vs. global minima — a bumpy landscape can have several dips. Gradient descent only ever looks at the slope right where it's standing, so it can settle into a shallow nearby dip (a local minimum) instead of the deepest dip on the whole surface (the global minimum).
  • Iterative updates over many steps — one nudge rarely reaches the bottom. Real training repeats this update thousands of times; one full pass over the training data is often called an "epoch," a term you'll see again later in the course.
