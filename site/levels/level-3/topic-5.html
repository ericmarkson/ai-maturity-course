Level 3 — Advanced Beginner

Perceptrons and Basic Network Architecture

Learning objective: You should be able to compute the output of a tiny perceptron by hand given inputs, weights, and a bias, and explain why a single perceptron can't separate a simple non-linear pattern like XOR but a multi-layer network can.

Definition

A perceptron is the simplest artificial neuron — it takes weighted inputs, sums them up, adds a bias, and fires a decision based on whether that sum crosses a threshold. Stacking many perceptrons in layers, where one layer's outputs feed the next layer's inputs, is the basic architecture of a neural network.

Subtopics

  • Inputs, weights, bias, and the weighted sum — each input is multiplied by a weight, summed together, and shifted by a bias term.
  • The activation/threshold step — the weighted sum is turned into a final decision (a simplified version of what full activation functions do, covered in Level 4).
  • Single-layer perceptron limits — a single perceptron can only draw a straight decision line, so it can't solve non-linearly-separable problems like XOR.
  • Why stacking layers overcomes that limit — a multi-layer perceptron can combine several straight lines to carve out much more complex shapes.
