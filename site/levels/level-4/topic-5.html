Level 4 — Intermediate

Evaluation Metrics: Precision, Recall, F1, ROC-AUC

Learning objective: Compute precision, recall, and F1 from a confusion matrix by hand, tune decision thresholds around asymmetric error costs (false positives vs. false negatives), and interpret ROC-AUC as a threshold-independent ranking metric.

Definition

Raw accuracy ((TP + TN) / Total) hides failure whenever classes are imbalanced or errors carry unequal costs. Classifier evaluation starts from the 2×2 confusion matrix (TP, FP, FN, TN) to separate alarm reliability (precision) from detection completeness (recall), combine them (F1), and evaluate score separation across all thresholds (ROC-AUC).

How It Works

1. Precision vs. Recall Trade-Off: Precision = TP / (TP + FP) measures how trustworthy a positive prediction is; Recall = TP / (TP + FN) measures what fraction of true positives the model actually caught. Raising the decision threshold makes the model flag fewer examples—boosting precision while dropping recall; lowering the threshold catches more positives at the cost of extra false alarms.

2. F1 Score (Harmonic Mean): F1 = 2 · (P · R) / (P + R). Because the harmonic mean is pulled down by whichever metric is smaller, a model cannot game F1 by achieving 100% precision with 5% recall (F1 = 0.095, unlike arithmetic mean 0.525).

3. ROC-AUC Across All Thresholds: Sweeping the decision threshold from 100 down to 0 traces True Positive Rate (Recall) against False Positive Rate (FP / (FP + TN)). The Area Under this ROC Curve (AUC) equals the probability that a random actual positive scores higher than a random actual negative (0.5 = random coin flip, 1.0 = perfect separation).

Worked Example: Computing Precision, Recall, and F1 by Hand

Suppose a spam filter evaluates 100 emails (10 real spam, 90 legitimate) at a chosen threshold and flags 8 emails as spam: TP = 6, FP = 2, FN = 4, TN = 88.

  • Accuracy: (6 + 88) / 100 = 94% (looks high, yet missed 40% of spam!)
  • Precision: 6 / (6 + 2) = 75% (3 out of 4 flagged emails were truly spam)
  • Recall: 6 / (6 + 4) = 60% (caught 6 of the 10 actual spam emails)
  • F1 Score: 2 × (0.75 × 0.60) / (0.75 + 0.60) = 0.90 / 1.35 ≈ 66.7%

Common Pitfall: Trusting ROC-AUC Blindly on Rare Events

When negatives vastly outnumber positives (say 99.9% legitimate transactions vs. 0.1% fraud), a tiny False Positive Rate like 1% still generates 10× more false alarms than true catches—wrecking precision even while ROC-AUC stays above 0.96. On heavily skewed data, inspect Precision–Recall curves and precision at fixed recall alongside ROC-AUC.
