Level 4 — Intermediate

Data Augmentation and Class Imbalance Handling

Learning objective: Explain why a classifier trained on a heavily imbalanced dataset (e.g. 90% one class, 10% the other) can look highly accurate while almost completely failing to detect the rare class, and describe how oversampling, data augmentation, and class weighting each address that problem differently.

Definition

Real-world datasets are often imbalanced — one class (say, "not fraud" or "healthy") vastly outnumbers another (say, "fraud" or "disease"). A model can reach very high accuracy just by mostly predicting the common class, while being nearly useless at the thing you actually cared about detecting. Data augmentation — creating realistic modified copies of existing training examples — and other rebalancing techniques exist to fix this without needing to collect an unrealistic amount of new labeled data for the rare class.

Subtopics

  • Why class imbalance breaks naive accuracy as a metric, and why recall on the minority class is often what actually matters
  • Data augmentation: generating new, realistic training variations from existing examples — image flips/crops/rotations/color jitter, text paraphrasing or synonym swaps, audio pitch/speed shifts — to increase effective data diversity without new labels
  • Oversampling the minority class (duplicating it, or augmenting it into new variations) vs. undersampling the majority class (throwing some of it away) — and why naive duplication risks overfitting to the exact duplicated examples
  • Class weighting: penalizing mistakes on the minority class more heavily in the loss function, as an alternative that changes training instead of the dataset itself
