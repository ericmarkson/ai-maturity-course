Level 4 — Intermediate

Transfer Learning

Learning objective: Explain why pretrained lower-layer representations transfer across tasks, choose between frozen feature extraction and progressive layer fine-tuning based on dataset size and domain similarity, and avoid overwriting pretrained features with overly large learning rates.

Definition

Transfer learning initializes a model for a new target task using weights learned on a large, general source task (e.g., ImageNet vision backbones or pretrained language models) instead of starting from random weights. Because early layers capture reusable low-level structure while upper layers specialize in high-level labels, reusing the backbone drastically shrinks required labeled examples and compute.

How It Works

1. Hierarchical Feature Reuse: In vision CNNs, Layer 1 detects oriented edges and color contrasts, mid layers build textures and geometric parts, and the final head maps parts to specific classes. In NLP, lower layers capture syntax and word sense while top layers align to task format.

2. Freezing vs. Fine-Tuning Spectrum:

  • Feature extraction (freeze backbone, train new head): Sets requires_grad = False on layers 1–4 and trains only a fresh Layer 5 classification head. Best when you have a small dataset (hundreds of examples) and source/target domains look similar.
  • Partial or full fine-tuning: Unfreezes later blocks (or all layers) once a warm head is trained, updating backbone weights gently when you have thousands of examples or subtle domain differences.

3. Source–Target Domain Distance: Natural photos transfer cleanly to wildlife ID or retail product tagging; distant domains (e.g., natural RGB photos → medical X-rays or ultrasound) need deeper unfreezing, and completely unrelated signals gain little from transfer.

Worked Example: Classifying 500 Manufacturing Defects

Suppose a factory collects 500 labeled photos of circuit-board defects across 4 classes:

  • Training a 25M-weight CNN from scratch: Severely overfits the 500 images (~60% val accuracy).
  • Frozen pretrained backbone + new 4-class head: Reuses pretrained edge/texture filters; fast to train and jumps to ~86% val accuracy.
  • Unfreezing only Layer 4 + Head (10× smaller lr): Adapts high-level object features to solder joints without scrambling universal edge detectors—reaching ~91%. Unfreezing Layer 1 on just 500 examples, by contrast, overfits general filters and drops validation accuracy.

Common Pitfall: Using Scratch-Training Learning Rates on Unfrozen Backbones

Starting fine-tuning with an uninitialized random classification head and a large learning rate (e.g., 1e-2) sends giant noisy gradients backward into carefully tuned pretrained layers, erasing useful features within epochs. Train the new head first (warm-up) or use a much smaller learning rate (like 1e-4 or 1e-5) on unfrozen backbone blocks.
