Intro to NLP: Tokenization, Embeddings, word2vec

Learning objective: Explain why "king − man + woman ≈ queen" is a meaningful demonstration of what an embedding space captures, and describe the difference between a one-hot word representation and a dense embedding.

Definition

Before a neural network can do anything with text, the text has to become numbers. Tokenization breaks raw text into small pieces (words or word-fragments). Embeddings turn each piece into a dense vector of numbers, positioned in space so that words with similar meaning end up near each other. word2vec is the classic algorithm that learns those vectors just by having the model try to predict a word from the words around it (or vice versa) across a huge amount of raw text — no human ever labels "these two words are similar."

Subtopics

  • Tokenization: splitting text into words or subwords a model can consume
  • Why raw text needs to become numbers, and why one-hot encoding every word is impractical (huge, sparse vectors that treat every pair of words as equally different)
  • Embeddings: dense, low-dimensional vectors where semantic similarity becomes geometric closeness
  • word2vec's core idea — predict a word from its neighboring context words, or predict the context from a word — as the classic historical technique. Modern systems use richer, context-aware embeddings built on the transformer ideas covered later in this course.
