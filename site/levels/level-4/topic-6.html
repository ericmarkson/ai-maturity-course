Level 4 — Intermediate

Regularization: Dropout, L1/L2

Learning objective: Diagnose overfitting from training vs. validation loss curves, compare how L1, L2, and dropout constrain model capacity during training, and navigate the bias–variance trade-off as regularization strength λ varies.

Definition

Regularization encompasses techniques that constrain a model during training so it discovers smooth, generalizable patterns instead of memorizing training noise. Because high-capacity neural nets and high-degree polynomials can drive training loss near zero by fitting every fluke, regularization trades a slight increase in training error for substantially lower validation and test error.

How It Works

1. L2 (Ridge) vs. L1 (Lasso) Weight Penalties: Both add a complexity term scaled by hyperparameter λ directly to the training loss: L_total = L_data + λ · Penalty(w). L2 adds ∑ w_i², shrinking large weights smoothly toward zero so no single feature dominates predictions. L1 adds ∑ |w_i|, pushing weak weights all the way to exact 0—acting as automatic feature selection.

2. Dropout (Stochastic Sub-Networks): On each training batch, dropout randomly zeroes out a fraction p (e.g., 20–50%) of hidden activations and scales survivors by 1 / (1 − p). Because neurons cannot rely on specific fragile partner neurons always being present, the network distributes redundant representations—much like averaging an ensemble of smaller models. During evaluation, dropout turns off.

3. Tuning Regularization Strength λ: Too little penalty leaves the model free to wiggle through noisy points (overfitting); too much penalty flattens weights toward zero so the model misses real structure (underfitting).

Worked Example: Sweeping L2 Penalty λ on a Noisy Curve

  • Weak penalty (λ = 10⁻⁶): An 11th-degree polynomial fits training points tightly (low train MSE) while oscillating wildly between points—causing validation MSE to spike.
  • Balanced penalty (λ ≈ 1–10): Large high-degree coefficients are penalized, smoothing the curve onto the true signal so validation MSE drops sharply.
  • Extreme penalty (λ = 1000): Weights shrink near zero, collapsing the curve into a nearly flat line where both training and validation MSE climb (underfitting).

Common Pitfall: Forgetting Feature Scaling Before L1/L2 & Leaving Dropout On

L1 and L2 penalize raw weight magnitudes equally—so if one feature is measured in millimeters (requiring tiny weights) and another in kilometers (requiring huge weights), regularization unfairly crushes the kilometer feature unless you standardize features first. In neural nets, also verify dropout is disabled during inference (e.g., model.eval()) so predictions aren't noisy.
