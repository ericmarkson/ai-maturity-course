Rigorous Eval Design: LLM-as-Judge and Statistical Significance

Learning objective: Identify at least one bias risk in an LLM-as-judge setup and explain why a 2-percentage-point difference on a 20-example eval set may not be statistically meaningful.

Definition

Rigorous eval design is the practice of building AI system evaluations that produce trustworthy, reproducible results — including using another LLM as an automated judge and applying statistical rigor to the resulting scores, so a difference in reported scores actually reflects a real difference in quality rather than sampling noise.

Subtopics

  • LLM-as-judge — using a model to score or compare outputs, and its known biases (position bias, verbosity bias, self-preference bias)
  • Calibrating judges — validating judge scores against human ratings, and rubric design
  • Statistical significance basics — sample size, confidence intervals, avoiding false conclusions from small eval sets
  • Regression test suites — tracking eval scores over time (tools like promptfoo, DeepEval as illustrative examples)
