Autonomous Agent Architecture: Planning, Self-Correction, Guardrails

Learning objective: Design a plan → execute → reflect loop for a sample autonomous task, and specify at least two guardrails that would prevent it from taking a harmful or costly action unsupervised.

Definition

Autonomous agent architecture is the overall design of how an agent breaks a goal into steps, checks its own work, and stays within safe operating bounds without constant human input. It's the difference between a model that answers one prompt and a system that pursues a multi-step goal on its own — deciding what to do next, noticing when something went wrong, and knowing when to stop and ask a human.

Subtopics

  • Planning strategies — task decomposition, plan-and-execute vs. purely reactive loops
  • Self-correction/reflection — the agent critiquing and revising its own output
  • Guardrails — input/output filters, permission scopes, human-in-the-loop checkpoints
  • Termination conditions — recognizing "done," or when to stop and escalate to a human
