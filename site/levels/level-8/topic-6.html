Multi-Modal Models: Vision-Language and Audio

Learning objective: Trace how images and audio waveforms are tokenized into embeddings inside a unified transformer, calculate the context-window token cost of high-resolution inputs, compare early/native fusion vs. cross-attention vs. dual-encoder designs, and prevent visual hallucinations caused by aggressive image downsampling.

Definition

Multi-modal models encode heterogeneous inputs — text subwords, image patches, and audio frames — into compatible vector sequences so a single Transformer backbone can reason jointly across modalities. Rather than piping outputs sequentially through fragile wrappers (like standalone OCR + text LLM), cross-modal models preserve spatial, visual, and acoustic nuances directly inside attention layers.

How It Works

Each sensory modality goes through a specialized front-end encoder and projection before entering the transformer:

  • Vision patch tokenization & ViT projectors: An image is sliced into P × P patches (commonly 14×14 pixels), flattened, and encoded by a Vision Transformer (ViT such as SigLIP/CLIP) into visual patch embeddings. A learned vision projector (2-layer MLP or spatial pixel-unshuffle pooling module) aligns those embeddings into the LLM's hidden dimension dmodel. For dense documents, dynamic high-resolution tiling splits large images into multiple sub-crops plus a global thumbnail.
  • Audio frame & codec tokenization: Speech/audio is either encoded from continuous log-mel spectrogram frames via a Whisper-style encoder + adapter, or discretized into neural audio codec tokens (RVQ) for native end-to-end audio-in / audio-out generation without intermediate ASR text loss.
  • Fusion architectures: Early / native fusion concatenates visual, audio, and text tokens into one sequence so full self-attention mixes them at every layer. Cross-attention fusion keeps text in the main stream and injects visual features via periodic cross-attention blocks (avoiding long KV sequences). Dual-encoder / late fusion embeds each modality independently for fast vector retrieval, but lacks fine-grained spatial reasoning.

Worked Example

Suppose a Vision-Language Model inspects a 1024 × 1024 financial table image using a 14×14-patch ViT with 2×2 spatial token pooling: 

Raw patches   = floor(1024 / 14)^2 = 73 * 73 = 5,329 patches
After 2x2 pool = 5,329 / 4         ~ 1,332 visual tokens

Adding a 60-second voice memo tokenized at 25 Hz audio frames adds another 60 × 25 = 1,500 audio tokens. Combined with a 300-token question, a single user turn consumes ~3,132 context tokens of prefill compute and KV-cache VRAM — explaining why multimodal latency and pricing scale tightly with image resolution and audio duration.

Common Pitfall

Low-resolution squashing & language-prior hallucination: Downsampling a dense PDF invoice or chart into a single fixed 336×336 square makes small digits illegible. Instead of reporting unreadability, the LLM falls back on statistical language priors — confidently hallucinating plausible-sounding totals or misbinding attributes across objects ("red cube left of blue sphere"). High-detail OCR/UI tasks require dynamic resolution tiling and aspect-ratio preservation.
