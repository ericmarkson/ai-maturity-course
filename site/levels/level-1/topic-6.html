Level 1 — Beginner

Basic AI Ethics: Bias and Privacy

Learning objective: Learner can give one concrete example of how biased training data could lead to an unfair AI outcome, and one example of a privacy trade-off in an everyday AI tool.

Definition

AI systems can inherit unfairness from their training data (bias) and can raise concerns about how personal data is collected and used (privacy) — two of the most immediate real-world consequences of AI to be aware of. Neither problem requires a system to be "broken": a model can work exactly as designed and still produce unfair outcomes or collect more personal data than most users realize.

Subtopics

  • Where bias comes from: skewed or unrepresentative training data
  • Real-world examples of biased outcomes (hiring tools, facial recognition, lending)
  • Privacy: what data AI systems collect and how it may be used or retained
  • Why "the AI decided it" doesn't remove human responsibility for outcomes

A model learns whatever pattern is in its training data — including patterns nobody intended to teach it. If a hiring model is trained mostly on résumés of people who were historically hired into a role, it can learn to favor whatever made those résumés look similar, even if that similarity has nothing to do with actual job performance. The model isn't "deciding to be unfair" in any deliberate sense; it is faithfully reproducing a skew that was already present in the data it was shown. This is why bias audits look at the training data and the outcomes, not just the code. Privacy raises a related but separate question: many AI tools work better the more personal data they collect (voice recordings, location history, browsing habits), which creates a real trade-off between convenience and how much of your data is gathered, stored, and potentially reused. And because a company, not the model, chose what data to use and decided to ship the system, responsibility for the outcome stays with the people who built and deployed it — "the algorithm did it" is not an excuse that makes accountability disappear.
