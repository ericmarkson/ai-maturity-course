Level 1 — Beginner

Narrow AI vs. General AI

Learning objective: Learner can explain, in one or two sentences, why a system like a chess engine or a chatbot is narrow AI even if it seems very capable in its domain.

Definition

Narrow AI (also called Artificial Narrow Intelligence, or ANI) is built to do one specific task, or a bounded family of tasks, well. Every AI system that exists today — no matter how impressive — is narrow in this sense. General AI (Artificial General Intelligence, AGI) is a still-hypothetical system that would match human-level reasoning and adaptability across essentially any task, including tasks it was never specifically built or trained for. Nobody has built one yet.

Subtopics

  • Artificial Narrow Intelligence (ANI) — task-specific systems like voice assistants, recommendation engines, and image classifiers
  • Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) — theoretical, human-level breadth and transfer across domains
  • Why today's large language models are still considered narrow despite feeling general in conversation
  • A brief mention of "artificial superintelligence" (ASI) — a further hypothetical stage beyond AGI, without dwelling on speculation

The trap to avoid is confusing "very good at a broad-feeling task" with "general." A modern chatbot can write code, draft an email, and discuss history in the same conversation, which feels general — but it's still operating within the boundaries of patterns it learned from training data and text-based interaction. It can't autonomously go learn to physically repair a car engine, run a business, or set its own goals the way a human easily adapts across wildly different domains. That gap between "broad within a lane" and "adaptable across any lane" is exactly what separates narrow from general.
